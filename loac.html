Marco Ciceri
Left Out in the Cold, Careless
Music: Liam Andrews
Video and lights: Marco Ciceri
Duration: 45'
A live audiovisual performance exploring the sonic and visual qualities of exposed, consumed, and corroded metal surfaces, through macro moving image, field recordings, and live sound processing. Metal is the material of industry: engineered to endure, to resist, to last. Yet it is also a silent witness. Every blow, every scratch, every trace of friction and corrosion remains inscribed on its surface, accumulating into a record of use and of time. What was built to stay unchanged becomes, under close observation, the very place where change is written and never erased.

The collaboration grows out of a shared interest in minimal and material aesthetics, particularly the relationship between macro visual textures and dense, physical sound. Both artists are drawn to processes of precision, repetition, and material transformation, and use performance as a space where these elements evolve together.

Using high-magnification lenses and controlled lighting, the surfaces of different metals are recorded up close, following the marks left by manufacturing, use, corrosion, and the passage of time. Presented in black and white, the moving image emphasizes texture, depth, and contrast, revealing details often imperceptible to the naked eye.

The sonic material is built from recordings of metal resonances, friction, impacts, and mechanical environments. Through live processing and composition, these recordings become an evolving sound environment in which the physical behaviour of the material forms the basis of the musical language. Sound is never accompaniment: image and sound develop together, shaping one another throughout the performance.

The work is driven by experimentation rather than predefined outcomes, letting the characteristics of the recorded materials guide both the visual and sonic composition. In this sense, Left Out in the Cold, Careless approaches sound as something untamed, emerging from physical matter rather than conventional musical structures. It unfolds as a continuous dialogue between image, sound, and material, listening to what the metal has kept.

Liam Andrews is a British-born, Australian-raised musician and visual artist based in Copenhagen. For the past decade, he has explored amplified guitar music with the experimental post-punk group MY DISCO, and more recently as part of EROS with Karl O’Connor (Regis, Sandwell District) and Boris Wilsdorf (Einstürzende Neubauten). Working within atonal and monochromatic aesthetics, his practice is shaped by place and the stark influence of Scandinavian winters; a reflection of solitude and the northern soundscape, sculpted into the brutalist form he is known for.
Under his solo moniker AICHER, Andrews focuses on repurposing incidental sounds within spatial recordings. Drawing from a vast archive of material, he creates compositions of feedback, unstable rhythm, and subharmonic pressure. His debut album, Defensive Acoustics (Downwards Records, 2025), was recorded in the basement of the Ishiguro Building, an abandoned pharmaceutical store in Kanazawa, Japan, capturing the reverberation and decay of the space itself. Andrews has performed at Berlin Atonal, Dark Mofo, Unsound, Le Guess Who?, Roadburn, Donaufestival, and Avant Arts, alongside extensive tours in Europe, the UK, North America, Japan, Southeast Asia, and Australia.
In 2023, he joined BIG BRAVE as a touring member and later became a permanent part of the group. He has also contributed to Ben Frost’s Scope Neglect (Mute Records, 2024) and Regis’s Hidden In This Is The Light That You Miss (Downwards Records, 2020).
Marco Ciceri is a Berlin-based Italian media artist and researcher working across live performance and installation. His practice combines sound, custom technologies, and moving image to investigate the hidden infrastructures of contemporary life through immersive audiovisual installations and performances. His work has been presented at Triennale Milano, the Barbican Centre, La Casa Encendida, and festivals including CTM, Berlin Atonal, MUTEK, and Terraforma.
Ciceri studied Design and Art at the Free University of Bozen-Bolzano, where his thesis explored the relationship between live and mediated reality. After collaborating with Marshmallow Laser Feast in 2015, he joined Berlin’s Pfadfinderei studio, creating visual content for internationally renowned artists, venues, and festivals.
Alongside his independent practice, he has developed long-term collaborations with musicians including Alessandro Cortini, Grand River, and Boris Wilsdorf (Einstürzende Neubauten). Known for his flowing aesthetic, Ciceri integrates generative software and motorised mechanics to create dynamic, detailed animations that blur the boundary between physical and digital.
