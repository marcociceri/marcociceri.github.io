Marco Ciceri
High Light
Light installation in collaboration with Axel Schoterman
High Light is a dialogue between two lights and two ages of light. One is a candle flame, the oldest artificial light humans have made. The other is its electric double, a light sculpture that reads every flicker of the fire and follows it in real time.
For millennia the flame was how we lit the night, and in every culture it has kept a ritual role, from Shinto shrines to Christian cemeteries. Watching a flame has long been a form of meditation. High Light places it beside the electric light we simply switch on, and invites the viewer to watch both the old way: slowly and attentively, as one watches a fire.
The electric light tries to learn the flame: its trembling, its flaring and its dimming. The emulation is close but never complete. In the gap between original and copy, the two lights sit in dissonance and harmony at once. The work reflects on how far human invention has travelled from its ancient traditions, and on what the newer light has gained and what it has lost.
High Light is also a work about time. As the candle burns down and its glow diminishes, its electric counterpart follows. The passage of time becomes visible in both lights at once. The work lasts as long as the wax, unless a visitor ends it first.
The interaction is as simple as it can be. The flame is the interface: every body passing by moves the air and bends the fire, and with it the electric light. There is no screen and there are no instructions. A breath is enough to change the whole installation, and a breath is enough to end it. If a visitor blows the candle out, both lights go dark and nothing relights them. High Light places this fragile power, and the responsibility that comes with it, in the hands of every visitor.

Marco Ciceri and Axel Schoterman are Berlin-based visual artists who share an interest in light, motion and the boundary between physical and digital.
Marco Ciceri (IT) is a media artist working across live performance and installation. Combining sound, custom technologies, motorised mechanics and moving image, he has presented work at Triennale Milano, the Barbican Centre, La Casa Encendida, Onassis Stegi and festivals including CTM, Berlin Atonal and MUTEK. After Marshmallow Laser Feast and Pfadfinderei, he developed long-term collaborations with Alessandro Cortini, Grand River and Boris Wilsdorf (Einstürzende Neubauten).
Axel Schoterman (NL) is a visual artist and director working with CGI and motion across live A/V and video installation. His work examines how we assign meaning and value to digital images. He has presented work for Triennale Milano, Draaimolen Festival and Anyma, with residencies at SIM (Iceland) and Aqtushetii (Georgia), and has been Art and Creative Director at Builders Club and Dada Projects, leading campaigns for Nike, Apple and McLaren.
